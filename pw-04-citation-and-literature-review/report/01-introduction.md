# Plagiarism and the importance of proper citation

**Plagiarism** is the use of someone else's words, ideas or research without proper attribution.

| Type | Description | How it is avoided in this work |
|------------|------------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Direct plagiarism | Copying another's work word for word | Quotations are marked and cited; text is written in own words |
| Self-plagiarism | Reusing one's own previous work without disclosure | The literature review in Task 3 is written anew; links to earlier works (PW3) are stated explicitly |
| Mosaic plagiarism | Borrowing phrases without quotation marks, mixing them with own text | Notes are made in own words before writing; every idea is linked to its source |
| Accidental plagiarism | Forgetting to cite or citing incorrectly | References are managed in a citation manager and inserted automatically |

Consequences of plagiarism include failing grades, retraction of publications, damage to reputation and legal consequences. Proper citation provides **acknowledgment** of the original authors, **validation** (readers can verify claims), **avoids misrepresentation** of others' ideas as one's own and maintains **academic integrity**.

# Overview of the citation styles

| Style | Field | In-text citation | Reference list | Distinctive features |
|---------|------------|------------------|---------------|------------------------|
| APA 7 | Social sciences, education, psychology | Author–date: (Karpukhin et al., 2020) | "References", alphabetical | Year after authors; sentence-case titles; DOI as URL |
| MLA 9 | Humanities | Author–page: (Karpukhin et al. 6770) | "Works Cited", alphabetical | Containers (journal, website); full first names; date near the end |
| Chicago 17 (notes and bibliography) | History, arts | Footnotes/endnotes | "Bibliography", alphabetical | Full notes on first citation, short notes afterwards |
| IEEE | Engineering, computer science, IT | Numbered in square brackets: [1] | "References", numbered in order of first citation | Initials before family name; abbreviations "vol.", "no.", "pp." |

Because the research topic belongs to information technology, **IEEE** is used for the reports of this course; APA, Chicago and MLA were practised in Task 1 and Task 2.
